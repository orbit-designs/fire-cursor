# Make the fire the page's only light

The Fire Cursor page has its best chance on Awwwards if it is built like a library or type-specimen site, where the effect is the hero and every section shows it working. It should not look like a product page with a demo tucked in a box. Awwwards has given Site of the Day to a smooth-scroll library (Lenis), a typeface specimen (Exat) and a creative-dev resource brand (Osmo), so single-concept pages can clearly win ([Awwwards: Lenis](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lenis-2), [Exat](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/exat-typeface), [Osmo](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/osmo)). Scoring is **Design 40%, Usability 30%, Creativity 20%, Content 10%**, so polish, speed and mobile quality count for more than novelty ([Hontran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)). The strongest references cover different parts of the job. Igloo Inc. shows a real-time intro and a particle system that reacts to links. landonorris.com shows a fluid-mask cursor reveal. Stefan Vitasović's 2025 portfolio shows a Swiss offset grid with shader grain. WebGL-Fluid-Simulation shows a live control panel and a working touch mode. The biggest risk is mobile: a cursor effect has no cursor on a phone, and the common shortcut of hiding the effect would remove the page's whole subject for mobile judges. One caveat on the evidence: awwwards.com blocked automated reading, so many Awwwards references below are confirmed only by title and URL. Dates, scores, fonts and colours for those pages still need a manual check, and **no verified 2024–2026 winner was found whose main subject is a fire or cursor effect**.

## Library and specimen sites show that one effect can carry a whole page

The confirmed SOTD winners closest to an open-source effect page are library and specimen sites. Lenis, darkroom.engineering's smooth-scroll library, has a page titled "Lenis - Awwwards SOTD" ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lenis-2)). The Exat typeface site, which presents one product the way a specimen presents a font, also carries the SOTD label ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/exat-typeface)). Awwwards also curates single-effect "Inspiration" items that work as a direct reference pool for cursor work, including "WebGL cursor animation effect – Sweetpunk", "Smoke space mouse effect – Torii Studio" and "Light power cursor interaction" ([Sweetpunk](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/webgl-cursor-animation-effect-sweetpunk), [Torii](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/smoke-space-mouse-effect-torii-studio-1), [Light power](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/light-power-cursor-interaction)). The "Hovers, Cursors and Cute Interactions" collection holds **440 items** ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/awwwards/collections/hovers-cursors-and-cute-interactions/)). A page called "Dracarys" also surfaced in a dark WebGL search. Its fire-and-dragon name makes it worth opening by hand, but its content and award are unverified ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/dracarys)).

The best-documented layout reference is Stefan Vitasović's 2025 portfolio. The author sits on the Awwwards jury. The layout is "inspired by Swiss print design", with "offset grid layouts with generous empty space" and deliberately unbalanced compositions. A welcome screen scrolls away to reveal the work, and one masked "characters-to-word" type animation repeats through the site as its visual hook ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/03/05/case-study-stefan-vitasovic-portfolio-2025/)). A fragment shader lays **noise grain and an LED overlay over every video** so all the visuals feel like one piece ([same](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/03/05/case-study-stefan-vitasovic-portfolio-2025/)). Shed.design, an SOTD winner reported in March 2026, follows the same idea of a curated sequence. It moves through project imagery with clip-path transitions, parallax, Lenis smooth scroll and a custom cursor ([Abduzeedo](https://abduzeedo.com/award-winning-web-design-how-sheddesign-earned-awwwards-sotd)).

Shed.design also shows the limit of this approach: custom cursors are now common on winning sites, so "a cursor that follows the mouse" will not stand out on its own. The parts of Fire Cursor that set it apart are the smoke that dissolves hovered UI and the liquid glass that reflects the fire. One award guide says winners each have **"one signature moment"** ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/award-winning-website-design-guide)), and another warns that "piling on interactions can read as a lack of control" ([Hontran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)). The smoke dissolve is the natural choice for that one moment.

## A black page, grain, and huge type give the fire something to burn

Evidence on palettes is thin, but it points one way. Awwwards has SOTD pages titled "Monochrome" and "MONOLOG", which suggests restrained palettes do well, although their actual colours were not verified ([Monochrome](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/monochrome), [MONOLOG](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/monolog)). Award guidance asks for a palette that is "intentional, cohesive, serving the brand identity" and does not say dark palettes win as such ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/award-winning-website-design-guide)). Lighting references support the idea of fire as a light source. Lusion's 2019 Site of the Month used a point light that follows the cursor across a dark pre-rendered scene, with the camera turning about 1.72° as the pointer moves ([Awwwards](https://awwwards.com/case-study-for-lusion-by-lusion-winner-of-site-of-the-month-may.html)). Iventions (2025), reported as an Awwwards SOTD and Developer Award winner, uses spotlight-style lighting that "treats each project like a lit installation" ([Hontran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)).

Together these support one visual rule: **the fire is the only colour and the only light on the page.** Keep the UI black, off-white and grey. Let UI elements pick up warm light only when the ring is near, and put a shader grain over the black so it looks like film instead of flat #000. The fastest way to find real black-and-ember winners is Awwwards' browse-by-hex filter, which works for any colour (example: [blue filter](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/%232C58FF/?page=4)). Run it with an orange hex.

For type, the confirmed pattern is custom or specimen-grade typefaces, sometimes several in one site. Shed.design rotates Owners, Sequel and Reckless, which gives a contrast between a serif and a geometric sans ([Abduzeedo](https://abduzeedo.com/award-winning-web-design-how-sheddesign-earned-awwwards-sotd)). Award guidance lists "custom type choices, a consistent scale, and readable body text" as traits of winners ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/award-winning-website-design-guide)). For Fire Cursor, oversized display type has a job to do: a huge wordmark gives the smoke something to dissolve and the glass something to reflect. Pair it with a monospace face for parameter readouts and code, which suits a library page. That pairing is a recommendation; it is not a proven winner trend.

## One idea per chapter, a real-time ignition, and embers that take the shape of links

Igloo Inc. (Awwwards case study, September 2024) is the most useful staging reference even though it has no cursor effect. It has **no separate preloader**. A real-time intro, rendered in the engine with custom shaders to keep file size down, flows straight into the experience ([Awwwards](https://awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html)). The landing page has three sections, each with one visual idea. Particles change colour with speed. Each external link forms its own particle shape. Scene transitions mix chromatic aberration, "tech displacement" and frost. Music and sound effects follow the particle movement "to make the interaction playful" ([same](https://awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html)). Igloo also scrambles text by offsetting an SDF texture in a shader, so it avoids expensive HTML/CSS restyling ([same](https://awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html)). Other winners pace their sections with GSAP so they feel like camera moves (Uncommon Studio) or a guided walk-through (Iventions) ([Hontran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)).

The closest match to the smoke-dissolve feature is landonorris.com. A November 2025 three.js forum thread describes its "liquid masking blob" cursor, which reveals an image underneath with soft, blobby edges. Replies suggest a WebGL fluid simulation used as a mask, or an adapted version of OGL's fluid distortion example ([three.js forum](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/how-to-recreate-this-liquid-blob-masking-cursor-effect-from-landonorris-com/87857)). Fire Cursor can use the same idea: the smoke buffer acts as an alpha mask that burns the UI layer away. Useful building blocks include Robin Delaporte's flowmap deformation technique ([GitHub](https://github.com/robin-dela/flowmap-effect)) and Codrops' September 2026 tutorial on custom-shaped cursor trails with Three.js and TSL ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/09/24/custom-shaped-cursor-trail/)).

Putting these references together gives a structure for the page. Each row below applies a verified pattern to Fire Cursor; the rows are design recommendations, not observed winners.

| Chapter | What the visitor sees | Reference pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Ignition intro | A spark lights into the ring on the first pointer move, with no percentage loader | Igloo's real-time intro |
| The ring | The cursor lights a near-black stage | Lusion and Iventions lighting in darkness |
| Trails and embers | Embers change colour with speed | Igloo's speed-based particle colour |
| Edge flames | On hover, embers gather into the button's outline | Igloo's links that form particle shapes |
| Smoke dissolve (signature moment) | The headline burns away to reveal content | landonorris.com fluid mask |
| Liquid glass | Glass cards bend and tint with the fire behind them | Vitasović's glass-like parallax |
| Playground and install | Live tuning, then copy the code | WebGL-Fluid-Simulation GUI |

Transitions between chapters can use a burn or char wipe in place of Igloo's frost. Microinteractions can be fire versions of known patterns: magnetic buttons whose edges catch flame ([Codrops, 2020](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2020/08/05/magnetic-buttons)), letters that flicker like embers before they settle (Osmo offers text scramble as a resource; [Osmo](https://osmo.supply/resource/text-scramble-load-scroll-hover)), and heat-haze distortion on headings near the cursor. Sound should be off by default if it is used at all. That is common practice, not something these sources state.

## A live control panel turns a demo into something people install

The clearest verified model for the playground is the control panel in the widely forked WebGL-Fluid-Simulation. It is a collapsible panel with grouped folders. It includes a quality dropdown (dye resolution **1024/512/256/128**), simulation resolution (32–256), sliders for diffusion, pressure and curl, "Bloom" and "Sunrays" folders, a pause toggle, a "Capture" folder with screenshot and transparent background, and a "Random splats" button that fires **5–24** bursts ([GitHub](https://github.com/hirajanwin/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation/blob/master/script.js)). The panel is **closed by default on mobile**, so the effect stays the hero ([same](https://github.com/hirajanwin/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation/blob/master/script.js)). Awwwards' Inspiration section also indexes shader playgrounds such as Shadertoy, ShaderFrog and The Book of Shaders, though these are older tools ([Shadertoy](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/shadertoy-build-and-share-shaders), [ShaderFrog](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/shaderfrog-the-shader-editor-for-three-js-without-writing-code)). Unicorn Studio shows the no-code, designer-facing side of the same idea ([Abduzeedo](https://abduzeedo.com/unicorn-studio-webgl-design-tool-designers)).

For Fire Cursor, the panel can follow this structure. Use folders for Ring, Trails, Embers, Smoke and Glass. Add named presets such as Candle, Campfire, Inferno and Blue flame, plus a quality selector, a random-burst button and a pause toggle. The most important control is **"copy config"**, which outputs the init snippet for the current settings. That step turns a visitor playing with the effect into someone who installs it, and it gives the 10% Content score real substance (docs, install, credits) instead of filler. Style the panel in the same monochrome mono type as the rest of the UI so the fire stays the only colour. One gap remains: no award-winning site with a cursor-effect configurator or copy-code button was found, and the layouts of library sites such as lenis.darkroom.engineering, gsap.com and theatrejs.com were not checked this session.

## On phones, drag to burn, tap to ignite, and let a ghost cursor fill the gaps

Mobile is where cursor showcases usually fail. Marketplace cursor components often just hide the effect. The Framer "Star Particle Cursor", for example, offers an "Option to hide the effect on mobile for performance" and describes no touch behaviour ([Framer](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/components/star-particle-cursor/)). For a page whose subject is the effect, that leaves mobile judges with nothing to see, and **Usability (30%) applies to mobile** ([Hontran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)). The Mobile Excellence badge, launched with Google in 2017, is scored by a panel against Google's mobile guidelines. Awwwards' own announcement set the threshold at **75/100** ([Awwwards](https://awwwards.com/google-and-awwwards-present-the-mobile-excellence-award.html)), while a 2026 secondary source says 70/100 ([Hontran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)). The current figure is unconfirmed. Vitasović's portfolio shows one accepted way to keep parity: drop WebGL on mobile but keep the same UI and motion language ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/03/05/case-study-stefan-vitasovic-portfolio-2025/)). For Fire Cursor that would remove the subject itself, so a touch version of the fire is the better path.

WebGL-Fluid-Simulation, which "works even on mobile", is the working model. It gives each finger its own pointer, so multi-finger trails work. It also fires **5–24 random splats on load**, so the screen is never empty before the first touch ([GitHub](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation)). Fire Cursor can build on that in three layers. First, a "ghost cursor" moves along a scripted path through the headline whenever no finger is down. It plays the role of the fluid sim's load-time splats, and it also shows the hover dissolve with no input at all. Second, while a finger is down, the ring follows it, with trail, embers and dissolve driven by finger speed. Third, a tap fires an ember burst. Tilt control can be an opt-in extra. On iOS it needs HTTPS and a `DeviceOrientationEvent.requestPermission()` call triggered by a user gesture such as a button click ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/Device_orientation_events)). No named award site was found using a ghost cursor or tap-to-ignite, so these are reasoned recommendations and should be tested on real devices.

The technical details decide whether this works. Use Pointer Events and branch on `pointerType` ("mouse", "pen" or "touch"). Handle `pointercancel`, which fires when the browser takes over a gesture as a scroll or zoom; after it, no more move events arrive ([javascript.info](https://git.jeena.net/jeena/en.javascript.info/raw/branch/master/2-ui/3-event-details/6-pointer-events/article.md)). The fluid sim calls `preventDefault()` on every touch over a full-screen canvas, which blocks scrolling ([GitHub](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation)). That is fine for a one-screen toy but would break a scrolling showcase. Put `touch-action: none` only on a bounded playground stage, and fade the ring out cleanly on `pointercancel` everywhere else. On the GPU side, iOS has had WebGL 2 since Safari 15 ([Khronos](https://www.khronos.org/blog/webgl-2-achieves-pervasive-support-from-all-major-web-browsers)), and EXT_color_buffer_float is reported at 100% on iOS ([web3dsurvey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgl2/extensions/EXT_color_buffer_float)). But linear filtering of 32-bit float textures (OES_texture_float_linear) is available on only **53.95% of iOS devices**, against 90.57% overall ([web3dsurvey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgl2/extensions/OES_texture_float_linear)). Copy the fluid sim's approach: use half-float render targets, test R16F, then RG16F, then RGBA16F, and fall back to a manual-filtering shader with bloom and shading turned off when linear filtering is missing ([GitHub](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation)). Size the hero with `100svh` rather than `dvh` (supported from Safari 15.4). `dvh` changes as the iOS address bar collapses, which would force costly framebuffer resizes ([OpenReplay](https://blog.openreplay.com/understanding-dynamic-viewport-units-css/)).

The "best on desktop" message is the least-documented area; no source covers how judged sites word it. The safest approach is to make the mobile version good enough that the note is only a hint. Show a small, dismissible line such as "Drag to light it up · Tap to ignite · Best with a mouse" only after the ghost cursor has already shown the effect, and never as a blocking screen.

## Jurors test at 4x CPU slowdown, so the fire needs quality tiers

A developer guide says jurors throttle the CPU 4x, simulate Fast 3G, look for about 60fps on mid-range phones, and check the reduced-motion fallback. This is presented as what jurors do, not an official Awwwards rule ([Hontran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/best-award-winning-websites-2026)). The same source warns that animation below 60fps costs more in Usability than it earns in Creativity ([Hontran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/awwwards-judging-criteria)). Winners deal with this by doing heavy work ahead of time and shipping small files. Igloo compressed its volume data below the size of "a typical website image" and compiled shaders and loaded textures in the background during the intro ([Awwwards](https://awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html)). Lusion shipped separate desktop and mobile assets: **4,096 vertices / 983KB against 1,024 vertices / 246KB** ([Awwwards](https://awwwards.com/case-study-for-lusion-by-lusion-winner-of-site-of-the-month-may.html)).

For the fire, the fluid sim gives a starting budget. It runs the physics grid at a fixed **128** and the dye at **1024**, dropping to **512** on mobile. Both are set independently of the screen's pixel count ([GitHub](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation)). It does not cap device pixel ratio, though, which is costly on 3x iPhones, so Fire Cursor should cap it at about 1.5–2 on touch devices. Drop the most expensive passes first (glass refraction, then bloom), and step down a quality tier if early frames run slow. Pause rendering when the stage is off-screen or the tab is hidden. Chrome's guidance says to "pause heavy background tasks when a component is not being rendered", using `IntersectionObserver` with a `rootMargin` of about 200px so work restarts just before the element scrolls into view ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/performance/efficient-background-processing)). Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, replace the autoplay and trails with a static, softly glowing ring and a visible "play" control. The device-specific numbers here (DPR cap, frame-time threshold) are recommendations to tune on real hardware; no benchmarks for fire or fluid effects on specific phones were found.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Awwwards is not short of cursor effects; the "Hovers, Cursors" collection alone has 440. What it rarely sees is a cursor effect presented with the discipline of a library site, and that is where the opening is. Fire Cursor already has three separate moves in the dissolve, the glass and the embers, so the risk is crowding rather than blandness. Making the smoke dissolve the single signature moment, and keeping fire as the only colour, turns a feature list into one clear concept, which is what the 40% Design weight rewards.

The less obvious finding is that mobile can become the page's advantage instead of its weak spot. Nearly every comparable component hides itself on touch, so a phone version where a ghost cursor burns through the headline before anyone touches the screen would set the page apart from its peers. The open items to check by hand before building are the colours and fonts of the "Monochrome", "MONOLOG" and "Dracarys" pages, Awwwards' orange colour filter for real black-and-ember winners, the current Mobile Excellence threshold, and the layouts of the Lenis and GSAP sites.
